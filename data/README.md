# Data

## Why MovieLens-1M

Before writing any model code I compared a few public datasets:

| Dataset | Size | Item metadata | Timestamps | Problem for this project |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MovieLens-100K | 943 users, 1.7k items, 100k ratings | genres, year | yes | too small for 10%-of-data sparsity runs and per-group cold-start numbers |
| **MovieLens-1M** | 6,040 users, 3.7k items, 1M ratings | genres, year, title + user demographics | yes | none that blocks the experiments (limitations below) |
| MovieLens-25M | 162k users, 62k items, 25M ratings | genres, tag genome | yes | full-graph GAT training and repeated runs do not fit a 6 GB GPU / 16 GB RAM |
| Amazon Reviews 2018 (one category) | depends, often 100k+ users | text, price, brand | yes | extremely sparse and noisy, the usual 5-core filtering removes exactly the cold-start cases I wanted to study |
| Gowalla / Yelp2018 (LightGCN versions) | 30-32k users | none in the common versions | partly | no content features, so no hybrid / item cold-start experiment |

MovieLens-1M was picked because it has enough interactions for graph learning, metadata for
both users (gender, age, occupation, zip) and items (genres, release year, title), timestamps
for a time-based split, and every experiment (including GAT with 3 seeds) runs on a laptop GPU.

## Raw statistics

Numbers from `results/tables/eda_summary.json` (generated by `scripts/run_eda.py`):

- 1,000,209 ratings from 6,040 users on 3,706 movies (177 of the 3,883 movies in `movies.dat`
  were never rated and are dropped)
- density 4.47% (sparsity 95.5%)
- ratings per user: median 96, min 20 (GroupLens only kept users with 20+ ratings), max 2,314
- ratings per movie: median 123.5, 446 movies have fewer than 10 ratings
- the 20% most rated movies receive 65% of all ratings
- ratings from 2000-04-25 to 2003-02-28
- no missing values, no duplicate (user, movie) pairs
- interaction type: explicit 1-5 stars only, mean 3.58

## Preprocessing

1. Ids are mapped to contiguous `user_idx` (0..6039) and `item_idx` (0..3705).
2. **Implicit feedback:** a rating of 4 or 5 is a positive (57.5% of ratings). Only positives
   are graph edges and training targets. Ratings of 1-3 are kept: the user has seen the movie,
   so it is removed from their recommendation candidates, and the ranking features use them
   (average rating, per-genre rating of a user). They are also allowed as sampled negatives.
3. Release year is parsed from the title, "Matrix, The" becomes "The Matrix".
4. Content vectors
   - items: 18 genre flags + standardised year + decade one-hot (11) + 16-d SVD of the title
     tf-idf = 46 dims
   - users: one-hot gender, age group, occupation and first zip digit = 40 dims

## Split

ML-1M users rate in one burst right after signing up: for 63% of users *all* ratings fall
within 24 hours of their first one, the median time between a user's first and last rating is
about an hour. A plain train/val/test split on time therefore puts mostly brand-new users in the
December 2000 validation window and mostly returning long-term users in the 2001-2003 test
window. Checking an 80/10/10 time split first: 611 of its 1,103 validation users had no training
history, but only 29 of its 1,188 test users (`results/tables/eda_extra.json`). Model selection would have optimised for a
different population than the one being tested, so the split is:

- one global time cutoff at the 80% quantile of all timestamps: **2000-12-02 14:52 UTC**
- training data = all 800,164 ratings before the cutoff (463,017 positives)
- the 1,762 users with at least one positive after the cutoff are split 50/50 into
  **validation** and **test** users, stratified by user group
- targets = each user's positives after the cutoff (112,264 in total), the seen-item filter =
  everything the user rated before the cutoff

Validation and test users come from the same period and population, nothing after the cutoff is
ever used for training, and the test users are only used for the final numbers.

| role | group | users | median training positives | median target positives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| val | warm (>20 training positives) | 488 | 107.5 | 23 |
| val | sparse (1-20) | 72 | 9 | 38 |
| val | cold (0) | 321 | 0 | 58 |
| test | warm | 487 | 103 | 26 |
| test | sparse | 73 | 6 | 28 |
| test | cold | 321 | 0 | 51 |

Item groups by training positives: 2,607 warm (>10), 862 sparse (1-10), 237 cold (0). Only 64
movies get their first positive after the cutoff, which is why item cold-start is studied with
a separate simulated split (`RecData.with_cold_items`, 10% of the catalog removed from training).

## Limitations of the data

- Explicit ratings turned into implicit feedback with a fixed threshold; a "3" is ambiguous.
- Most users rate their whole history in one session during sign-up, and MovieLens shows
  popular movies first during that process. Some of the popularity bias in the targets comes
  from the interface, not only from taste.
- Content is thin: 18 genres, a year and a title. There are no plots, cast or tags.
- One country/period (US website, 2000-2003), demographics are self-reported categories.
